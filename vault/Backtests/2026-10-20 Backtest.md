# 2026-10-20 Backtest

- On average a stock beat the S&P 500 by **+0.9%** over the 3 months after an insider purchase was filed (95% CI -0.1% to +2.0%, 100,972 signals since 2006). That is roughly +3.5% a year, but the median stock *lagged* by 0.9%.
- The edge was real in **2006-2015** (+7.9% a year, p 0.006) and has **faded since 2016** (+1.1% a year, p 0.763).
- The best simulated portfolio (*Insider buying after a 30%+ run-up (post-hoc)*) returned +20.2% a year after costs with a 40% max drawdown, against SPY's +11.0% and 55%.
- Survivorship: about half of all insider buys are in companies that no longer trade and can't be priced. If those did like a typical priced buy, the average falls from +0.9% to -0.1%; if they did badly, to -12.6%.
